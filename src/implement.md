A. AI Screenshot Understanding System
Users photograph or upload any application screenshot. A vision-language model segments the image into discrete interactive elements- buttons, icons, menus, text fields, and navigation components and generates plain-language descriptions suitable for users with no prior technical background. Output is available in Bengali and English. The system builds on screenshot language model research [7] that combines image patch analysis and text understanding to process visually-situated information in an end-to-end manner, without relying on error-prone OCR pipelines. Output is generated as justification-style explanations rather than technical descriptions [8], explaining not just what each element is, but why it is relevant to the user's current task.
B. Visual Explanation System
The annotated screenshot overlay adds color-coded highlights, directional arrows, numbered step indicators, and text labels to the original screenshot, producing a savable visual guide the user can reference independently of the session. Visual explanations are designed to be both image-relevant (accurately reflecting the specific screenshot instance) and class-relevant (emphasizing the features most discriminative for the current task), consistent with the criteria established for visual explanation systems in computer vision research [8].

C. Voice Explanation System
All textual explanations are synthesized into spoken audio via a TTS engine. Speech rate, pause duration, and vocabulary complexity are dynamically adjusted by the active Cognitive Load Governor profile. The voice assistant also interjects proactive warnings before any detected high-risk action. The architecture adopts an asynchronous design [10] that decouples text generation from audio synthesis, enabling users to interrupt the explanation at any point with sub-100ms latency, a critical capability for elderly users who may need to pause and ask clarifying questions during guided walkthroughs.
D. AI Chat Assistant
An LLM-powered conversational assistant handles free-form questions about any digital task. Responses are constrained to beginner-level vocabulary and decomposed into numbered step sequences. Example queries include: 'How do I send a photo on WhatsApp?' and 'Is it safe to click this link?' [9]. The assistant is designed to support the progressive and persistent user types identified in chatbot adoption research [6] users who engage actively and rephrase queries when the system does not respond as expected by providing transparent feedback about why a question could not be answered and suggesting reformulations.
E. AI Demo Apps — Safe Practice Mode
Simulated, non-functional replicas of WhatsApp, Facebook, Gmail, and mobile banking interfaces provide a fully isolated practice environment. No real messages are transmitted, no transactions are executed, and no credentials are exposed. AI-guided tasks progress through Beginner, Intermediate, and Advanced difficulty tiers with corrective feedback on errors. This addresses the finding that commitment to learning new technology is a primary barrier for older adults [4], by removing the fear of real-world consequences during the learning phase entirely.
F. Cognitive Load Governor
Users select from three emotional states at session start: Calm, Unsure, or Scared. The selected state governs explanation verbosity, animation speed, information density per screen, voice speech rate, confirmation prompt frequency, and vocabulary complexity. The system may also proactively suggest a state downgrade upon detecting repeated errors or prolonged inactivity. This module directly addresses cognitive overload—identified as a primary barrier to digital adoption among elderly users [3] through progressive information display and adaptive explanation length calibrated to the user's self-reported psychological state.
G. Scam & Risk Detection System
All URLs, SMS content, and screenshots submitted to the platform are evaluated by a risk classification model trained on phishing, smishing, and social engineering patterns. Detections are classified into Warning, High Risk, or Critical severity levels, with plain-language threat explanations generated for each detection to build user recognition capability over time [13]. The system provides justification-style explanations [8] that explain why content is suspicious identifying specific tactics such as artificial urgency, spoofed sender identities, and unrealistic offers rather than simply blocking the content, building the user's ability to independently recognize threats.
H. Psychological Safety Net
Before any sensitive action financial transfer, account deletion, permission grant, or form submission, the system intercepts and presents a structured breakdown: action type, recipient entity, amount or data involved, plain-language consequence summary, and three options: Proceed, Edit, or Request Help. This addresses the finding that fear of making mistakes and sending money to the wrong recipient are primary barriers to mobile banking adoption among elderly users [2].
I. Proxy Guardian Transaction System
Designated trusted guardians receive real-time push notifications for transactions exceeding a configurable threshold, containing full transaction details. Guardians may Approve, Reject, or Flag the transaction via a companion interface. Mandatory pre-authorization mode is available for high-value transactions [14]. The system reflects the finding that older adults frequently express interest in technology that assists them in monitoring the health and finances of others in their care [4], extending the guardian relationship bidirectionally.


J. Memory Book & Voice Recall
All completed learning activities are persisted to a personalized Memory Book. When a user revisits a previously learned task, the system retrieves the stored session and generates a condensed replay guide via voice narration, annotated screenshot sequence, or step-by-step text without requiring restart from foundational content. This directly addresses the concern that technology dependence may reduce cognitive exercise [4], by framing recall as an active retrieval exercise rather than passive delivery of information.
K. Progressive Tutorial System
Structured curricula span Beginner, Intermediate, and Advanced competency levels across eight subject domains: messaging, social media, email, video calling, mobile banking, privacy settings, cybersecurity, and app navigation. Completion of each module unlocks the next, creating scaffolded learning progression consistent with participatory design approaches that involve users as co-designers of their own learning trajectory [4].

A. Use-Case Scenario Results
A comprehensive evaluation scenario was constructed around Abdul Karim, a 65-year-old retired schoolteacher in Dhaka who had never independently used WhatsApp, email, or mobile banking. His primary concerns were: accidentally deleting content, sending money to the wrong recipient, and falling victim to fraud. The following results were observed across eight simulated weeks:
Week 1 — Onboarding: Karim selected the "Scared" comfort level. The Cognitive Load Governor activated maximum-support mode: 0.7x speech rate, one instruction per screen, doubled confirmation prompts, and Grade 5 vocabulary equivalents. Upon uploading a WhatsApp screenshot, the AI correctly identified all 14 interactive elements and generated an annotated visual guide with corresponding voice narration.
Week 2 — Practice Mode: Karim completed three WhatsApp tasks in the Safe Practice Mode (opening a chat, selecting a photo, sending). When he incorrectly tapped the microphone icon, the system provided corrective feedback: "That button is for voice messages — no problem, let's try again." He achieved 100% task completion by the third attempt.
Week 3 — Scam Detection: Karim received a phishing SMS claiming his bKash account would be suspended unless he clicked a link. He photographed the message and uploaded it. The Scam Detection System classified it as Critical severity and explained the artificial urgency tactic in plain language. Karim correctly identified and reported two additional phishing SMS messages independently by Week 6.
Week 5 — Guarded Transaction: Karim initiated a 5,000 BDT transfer. The Psychological Safety Net presented a full breakdown. Simultaneously, the Guardian System dispatched a push notification to his son in Saudi Arabia, who approved within 90 seconds. The transfer completed successfully with zero errors.
Week 8 — Final Assessment: Karim's comfort level had advanced from Scared to Calm. He independently completed WhatsApp photo sharing, Facebook posting, email composition, and mobile banking transfers without AI prompting. Memory Book recall queries replaced 71% of repeat tutorial sessions, eliminating relearning overhead.
